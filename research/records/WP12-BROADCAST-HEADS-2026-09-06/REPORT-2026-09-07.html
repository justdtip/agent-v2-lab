Do lens-relaying attention heads carry workspace content? A reproduction and causal test of the "broadcast heads" claim on Qwen3.5-4B

Interpretability programme, work package 12 · run on 5–6 September 2026 on one Apple-silicon laptop · written up 7 September 2026 from the committed record research/records/WP12-BROADCAST-HEADS-2026-09-06

Abstract

A recent paper proposes that a language model keeps a "workspace" in its middle layers, readable through a fitted linear probe (the J-lens), and that a small set of attention heads, the "broadcast heads", carries workspace content: heads whose output weights relay the probe's own directions strongly and faithfully. We asked whether such heads exist on Qwen3.5-4B, a hybrid model with 24 recurrent and 8 attention blocks, and whether they do what the paper says. By weight arithmetic alone we find a real, reproducible population: 26 of the 80 in-band attention heads relay lens directions selectively against three families of controls, against 3 of 32 heads outside the band, and the population survives removing the model's output direction from the probe. The paper's gain criterion does not transfer, its prediction that relays concentrate in the first half of the band is refuted on five scorings, and a hypothesis that the out-of-band relays feed the band is unsupported by weights and by intervention. Causally, the picture is mixed. Ablated together, the top two relays damage the lens readout as much as nine random heads at the same layers, but ablated one at a time no relay is stronger than the other heads at its layer, the set's effect is strongly redundant, and an injection experiment built to decide whether the set carries workspace content specifically could not be made sensitive, because content added to the residual stream reaches later layers through the skip connection whatever heads are removed. We also find that the probe itself is a narrow filter at the start of the band and largely an output readout at its end, leaving one layer where it is both wide and independent. The population is established; the paper's causal claim is neither supported nor refuted on this model, and the experiment that would decide it is named.

1. Background

The claim under test. The paper fits, for each layer of a transformer, a linear map (the J-lens, a Jacobian averaged over many prompts) from that layer's residual stream to the model's final output, so that any middle-layer state can be "read" as a distribution over vocabulary tokens. Layers where this reading is stable and predictive form a "workspace band". The paper then scores every attention head by how strongly and how faithfully its output circuit relays the lens's own directions, calls the top one percent "broadcast heads", reports that they concentrate in the first half of the band, that ablating them drops what the lens can recover downstream (recall at 25 from 0.86 to 0.67 against random heads) while barely changing the model's output, and that they are what moves workspace content between positions.

Our model and its band. Qwen3.5-4B has 32 blocks: 24 gated-DeltaNet recurrent blocks and 8 attention blocks, the attention blocks writing layers 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28 and 32. The programme's earlier work fixed the workspace band as five layer pairs, 12/13, 16/17, 19/20, 23/24 and 27/28, whose attention members are layers 12, 16, 20, 24 and 28: five blocks of sixteen query heads, eighty in-band heads in all. A hosted J-lens fitted to this model already existed and had been checked against a full-precision copy of the model; the probe was used on the 4-bit checkpoint throughout.

The questions. (Q1) Do heads that relay lens directions exist on this model, and by what criterion? (Q2) Where do they sit, and do they concentrate in the first half of the band? (Q3) The Director's hypothesis: relays found outside the band, at layers 4 and 8, are coupling heads that feed the band. (Q4) The paper's causal claim: do the relays carry workspace content, such that removing them removes it?

2. Methods

2.1 Scoring heads by weight arithmetic

An attention head's effect on the residual stream, ignoring where it attends, is the product of its value projection and its slice of the output projection: a 2,560 by 2,560 map. For each of the 128 attention heads and, for comparison, each of the 768 recurrent value paths, we applied this map to a population of 2,000 lens directions at the block's input layer (the directions the lens reads as 2,000 sampled vocabulary tokens) and asked two things: gain, the mean length of the images relative to random directions; and label preservation, whether each direction's image is nearer to itself than to any other member of the population, summarised as a mean reciprocal rank. Three controls: the same population rotated by twenty random orthogonal matrices, twenty samples of rows from the block's MLP output weights, and random unit directions. A head relays the lens selectively if its preservation on the lens population exceeds every one of the forty null draws and is at least double their medians. Layer 32, the output layer, is excluded because the lens there is the output map itself.

Because the lens direction of a token correlates with that token's own output embedding, and the correlation grows with depth (section 3.6), every result was recomputed on an orthogonalised population from which each direction's component along its token's embedding had been removed. A tiny-number problem in the ratio rule was closed with an absolute floor of 0.1 on preservation, and every rule change was followed by re-scoring every prediction with all scorings shown.

2.2 The coupling hypothesis

For the three layer-4 relays, we computed how strongly their output maps compose with the query, key and value weights of every band head, against a rotation null, and separately whether the layer-4 relays preserve lens content, token identity, or both. Causally, each of the sixteen layer-4 heads was zeroed alone on real text and the change it caused in the outputs of the 26 in-band relays was compared with the change it caused in the 54 other in-band heads.

2.3 Graded ablation

The in-band relays were zeroed together, k at a time in order of absolute preservation (k = 2, 4, 8, 16, and the whole sets left by each floor), on eight documents of 512 tokens at 64 positions each, and the lens was read at all ten band layers. The readout statistic is the share of the unablated readout's top-25 tokens that survive at the layers downstream of an ablated head; it equals 1 by construction with nothing ablated, so it measures damage to the model's prior output rather than recall of anything external, and is not the paper's recall at 25. Each arm was compared with five layer-matched random head sets of the same size drawn from the unselected heads, with a bit-identical reproducibility pass before any reading and a hard gate that readouts upstream of every ablated head equal the unablated readout exactly. A pre-registered specificity rule required the selected set's next-token change rate to stay within twice the random sets'. Each arm was also expressed as the number of random heads at which the random curve reaches the same damage, interpolated between the two measured arms that bracket it.

2.4 Single-head sweep

Every one of the eighty in-band heads was zeroed alone on the same documents, with damage read at layer 28, the one readout downstream of every head, and over all ten layers for sums. The pre-registered statistics were the rank correlation between preservation and single-head damage over the eighty, within the 26 relays and within the 54 others, and a test of redundancy: the measured k-arm overlap against the product of the top-k single-head overlaps, which is what independent effects would give.

2.5 Group structure

Sixteen query heads share four value projections. To ask whether the relay property lives in the shared projection, we pre-registered a one-way variance ratio of preservation between and within groups over the sixteen real heads, compared with the same ratio computed on each of the forty null populations under the same heads, so that the null is the group structure the real heads impose on any population. Secondary probes composed every output slice with every value projection, scored each head's own slice against twenty scrambled correspondences with its own projection, and measured the share of the lens population's energy inside each projection's row space through the orthogonal projector (isotropic expectation 0.10).

2.6 Injection

A direction the lens reads as a chosen concept token was added to the residual stream at a chosen layer and position, and the lens was read downstream, at the same position at every deeper band layer and at later positions, with and without the downstream relays zeroed, against nineteen layer-matched random sets. Three instruments were built. The gradient direction registered too weakly; the exact pre-image of the lens, at a condition number of 636,000, produced an invisible injection; a regularised pre-image at twice the residual norm registered at layer 12, and a contrast direction, the concept's row minus the mean of its competitors, registered at once the norm at layer 20. The reading rules, the primary comparison and what each outcome would mean were fixed before each arm was read; a comparison in which the nineteen random arms and the relay arm all sit within one event of the unablated value was ruled uninformative in advance.

2.7 Discipline

Every rule was pre-registered before its data were read, every change of rule was recorded with a re-scoring, and each experiment's instrument had to prove it measured what it claimed (a reproducibility pass, a reconstruction gate, a calibration) before its readings were read. Results were reviewed as they arrived by a second session acting as the head of interpretability and a third acting as the research division, whose corrections are part of the record.

3. Results

3.1 A real, reproducible population of relays

The paper's gain criterion does not transfer: no head's output map amplifies lens directions beyond its own rotated controls, and gain and preservation select different heads (17 pass both, 41 pass preservation alone). Selective preservation does transfer. On the orthogonalised population, 29 of the 112 scored attention heads pass, 26 of the 80 in the band against 3 of the 32 outside it, a ratio of 3.5. Only 19 of the 768 recurrent value paths pass and none is stronger than the median attention relay, so the property is an attention property. Where they sit. The paper predicts concentration in the first half of the band. Scored as rates per head on the five-layer band, the first half (layers 12, 16, 20, 48 heads) holds 0.21 of its heads as relays and the second half (24, 28, 32 heads) 0.41; the prediction is refuted on every rule used, five scorings in all. Layer 28 is saturated, 9 of 16, and this is a property of its heads rather than of the test: its null draws are not lower than layer 20's. Relays also cluster by key-value group at three of the five band layers (section 3.5).

3.2 The coupling hypothesis is unsupported

The three layer-4 relays compose with band heads no more than rotated controls do: none of 192 pairings exceeds the pair population's 99th percentile on the query, key or value channel. They preserve lens content (0.10 to 0.16) and token identity at random, so they carry content, not addresses. Causally, zeroing each alone changes the band relays' outputs by a median of 0.072 against 0.058 for the other thirteen layer-4 heads (one-sided p = 0.12), and every layer-4 head, relay or not, perturbs the unselected band heads more than the relays (selectivity 0.89, 0.77 and 0.91 for the relays against 0.74 to 0.92 for the rest). Not a coupling; a slightly larger general influence.

Random ablation of any two heads already costs ten points; the top two relays cost sixteen, the damage of nine random heads. The cumulative factor falls with k, but that is arithmetic: per head, ranks three to eight add about half of what ranks nine to sixteen add, so the ordering by preservation is not monotone in effect. The redundancy test is decisive on one point: at every k the measured overlap sits above the product of the single-head overlaps (0.722 against 0.538 at k = 16), so the relays damage overlapping content. Heads that damage the same content are heads carrying the same content, which is what broadcast would look like from outside, and also what general influence on the same readout would look like.

3.4 Ablated alone, no relay is special

Over the eighty heads the rank correlation of preservation with single-head damage at layer 28 is −0.17 (p = 0.13); within the 26 relays 0.09 (p = 0.65); within the 54 others −0.31 (p = 0.022). Within every layer the relays are level with or weaker than the unselected heads (medians, relays against others: layer 12, 0.109 against 0.091; 16, 0.061 against 0.091; 20, 0.063 against 0.061; 24, 0.052 against 0.055; 28, 0.067 against 0.061). Seven of the ten single heads that damage the readout most preserve no lens directions at all. The pooled correlation is confounded by source depth and the within-layer comparison carries the argument. The readouts upstream of every ablated head were exactly equal to the unablated readout in all eighty cases.

3.5 Group structure without a mechanism at the projection level

The pre-registered variance ratio exceeds all forty null draws at layers 12, 24 and 28 (at 28, 2.83 against a null maximum of 1.81) and not at 16 or 20 (at 20, 2.45 against a null median of 2.50, where one group holds three near-identity "copy" heads that preserve any population). So heads sharing a value projection preserve the lens alike at three of five blocks. But the scale-free energy share says the projections do not carry the lens directions differentially: at layer 28 the group holding all four relays captures 0.115 of the lens population's energy and the group holding none 0.105, against an isotropic 0.10. The cross table pairs every output slice with every projection and only the real pairings ever pass; the pairing control is beaten by 25 of 26 relays and by 36 of 54 non-relays, a difference that is confounded with the selection. The group structure of preservation is real and has no mechanism at the level of the value projection's alignment with the lens.

3.6 The injection instrument cannot test broadcast at the same position

Two working instruments, at layer 12 and at layer 20, gave the same shape. At the primary readout (layer 24) every one of nineteen random arms and the relay arm sat within one event of 64 of the unablated value: no head set of any kind removed injected content there, and the comparison is uninformative by the pre-registered rule. At the output-aligned readout (layer 28) the first instrument showed the relays' removal raising recall by four events (rank 19 of 20, one-sided p = 0.10), and the second, with a less foreign injection, by one event with the random range shrinking from nine events to two. At every later position, on every arm, the injected concept was never read. The reason is structural: same-position transport across layers is the residual stream's skip connection, so removing heads can add to injected content or overwrite it but never remove it, and the cross-position readout, where broadcast would show, carries no signal for this instrument.

Why redundancy does not settle it. If several heads write the same workspace material, removing any one changes little and removing the set changes a great deal: exactly the ablation curve and the single-head null we observe. The same pattern arises if several heads each exert general influence on the same readout. The one experiment that separates the readings has to add known content and ask whether the set is needed to carry it somewhere, and on this model that "somewhere" cannot be the same position, because the residual stream carries it there regardless. The paper's own instrument, an injected-thought report protocol that reads the model's generation at later positions, is cross-position by construction and is the discriminating experiment.

Copy maps. Four separate measurements in one night were confounded by heads whose output map is close to a scaled identity: they preserve any population's labels, capture any population's energy, and impose group structure on anything. The programme now requires every preservation-type statistic to carry a control against arbitrary populations under the same maps. The layer-20 group result and the energy outlier are the two cases that would otherwise have read as findings.

The probe. Both findings about the lens (Table 3) change what every lens reading in the programme means. A claim at layer 12 inherits a narrow filter; a claim at 28 is two-thirds a claim about the output. Layer 20 is now the programme's primary readout, and results at the other layers are reported as a profile with both defects stated per row.

Relation to the paper. The population reproduces, the selection criterion does not, the spatial prediction fails, and the causal claim is untested rather than refuted. Two differences deserve weight: this is a hybrid model in which the recurrent channel carries a running summary rather than addressable content, so attention's role may differ from a pure transformer's; and our ablation metric is damage to the model's own prior readout, not recall of an externally known target, so the paper's 0.86 to 0.67 and our curve are not the same quantity.

5. Limitations

  • One model, one checkpoint precision. The 4-bit and full-precision lenses agree except at layer 16, which is reported separately by rule.
  • Small text sample for the causal work: eight documents, 64 positions each; the ablation controls have five seeds and the injection controls nineteen.
  • Sixteen heads in four groups cannot settle group membership by count; the weight test carries that result.
  • The injection magnitude at layer 12 was twice the residual norm, larger than the whole residual at its position; conclusions from that instrument inherit it. The concept tokens were multilingual word pieces from a vocabulary filter.
  • Rank correlations within a layer rest on sixteen heads.
  • The comparison of relays with random sets at the same layers is fair at each size, but the "equivalent random heads" figure crosses arms with different layer mixes and is reported with its bracketing arms for that reason.

6. Conclusions and next experiments

On Qwen3.5-4B the paper's broadcast heads exist as a real and reproducible population of attention heads that relay the lens's directions selectively, favoured in the workspace band by a factor of three and a half and organised in part by shared value projections. Nothing measured shows them carrying workspace content specifically: individually they are ordinary, as a set they matter redundantly, and the experiment that would separate content-carrying from general influence needs a cross-position readout that a lens-injection, lens-readout design cannot provide. Three experiments follow directly: the paper's injected-thought report protocol with the relays ablated as a set; a lens readout at later positions on content the model demonstrably propagates; and the recall of the model's own final token under ablation, the paper's other metric. Each should read at layer 20 first, where the probe is both wide and independent.

Glossary

Residual stream
The running vector, 2,560 numbers per token position here, that every block reads from and adds to; the model's working state. A block's contribution is added to it, which is why content placed there persists through later layers by default.
Attention head
One of sixteen sub-units of an attention block. Each reads earlier positions, mixes their values, and writes the result back through its own slice of the output weights. The heads here share four "value" projections in groups of four.
J-lens, lens direction
A fitted linear map from a middle layer's residual stream to the model's final output, used as a probe. The lens direction of a token is the residual-stream direction the probe reads as that token.
Workspace band
The layers where the lens reading is stable and predictive; here the layer pairs 12/13, 16/17, 19/20, 23/24 and 27/28.
Label preservation, mean reciprocal rank
Apply a head's map to a set of directions; for each, rank the original directions by closeness to the image; preservation is the average of one over the rank of the correct original. One means every image is nearest its own origin.
Ablation
Zeroing a head's output during a forward pass and measuring what changes downstream.
Layer-matched random set
A control: the same number of heads drawn at random from the unselected heads at the same layers, so the comparison holds depth constant.
Top-25 overlap
The share of the twenty-five tokens the lens ranks highest that are still in its top twenty-five after an intervention. One by construction with no intervention.
Wilson interval
A 95 percent confidence interval for a proportion that behaves well for small counts.
Spearman rank correlation
Correlation of the orderings of two quantities rather than their values; one when the orderings agree, zero when unrelated.
Pre-registration
Writing down the statistic and the reading rule before the data are looked at, so that a result cannot be constructed after the fact.
Condition number
The ratio of a linear map's largest to smallest stretching factor; a large number means the map nearly collapses some directions, and its inverse blows them up.
