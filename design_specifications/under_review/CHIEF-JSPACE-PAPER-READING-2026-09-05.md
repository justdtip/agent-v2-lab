# Chief's reading of Gurnee, Sofroniew et al., "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models" (arXiv 2607.15495), and what it licenses for Qwen3.5-4B

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: Chief AI Research Scientist. Status: discussion for the Director, written under the standing veto. Nothing in this memo is ratified, dispatched, or run. Every quantity attributed to the paper below was read from the paper's own text (main sections 1 to 9 and appendices A.6 to A.8, A.12, A.14 to A.20 and A.24, read verbatim from the arXiv HTML on 2026-09-05), not from a summary.

## 0. The Director's ask, echoed back

1. Read the paper mechanically and say to what degree this model, Qwen3.5-4B, has a J-space, with the recollection that the paper finds none in roughly the first third of layers.
2. The purpose is long-context agentic behaviour: recalling early steps of a plan and early decisions, which the Director calls a prerequisite of agentic work and the goal.
3. Thread 1: the assistant axis inhabits the J-space and post-training instills that access; hypothesis that goal retention is an assistant-axis matter because the assistant pursues goals the user wants; ask whether the model retains goals and whether we can strengthen what it emphasises internally.
4. Thread 2: attention emphasises J-space activations roughly ten times more than other content; if the model can be made to represent long-horizon goals in the J-space preferentially, agent training could be far more efficient.

Two of these premises need correction against the text. They are corrected in sections 5 and 6, and the correction changes what experiments are worth running, not whether the threads are worth pursuing.

## 1. The instrument, exactly

- The averaged Jacobian: J_l = E over prompts, source positions t and target positions t' >= t of dh_final,t' / dh_l,t. The reference fit uses 1000 sequences of 128 tokens from a pretraining-like corpus, injects a one-hot cotangent at every valid target position at once and backpropagates, skips the first 16 positions as attention sinks, averages by a plain mean, targets the final layer by default, and stores J in float16. Applying the lens replaces every layer above l with J_l, then the model's own final norm and unembedding: lens(h) = softmax(W_U norm(J_l h)).
- J-lens vectors are the rows of W_U J_l, one direction per vocabulary token. They are overcomplete (n_vocab > d_model) and span the residual stream, so the J-space is not a subspace. It is the union of the k-dimensional cones of nonnegative combinations of k J-lens vectors, with k typically at most 25 (A.8). The J-space component of an activation is found by gradient pursuit. That component carries a small share of activation variance: never more than 10% in excess of a random control at the workspace layers (section 4.2), 6 to 7% of a concept vector's variance (3.1), 10 to 15% of a two-hop intermediate probe's variance (3.3).
- Variants (A.7): penultimate-layer target; frozen-QK (gradients through query and key projections zeroed, so only the value pathway contributes, "what gets moved" versus "what gets attended to"); self-only (t' = t, closest in spirit to the logit lens); future-only (t' > t, "isolates the broadcast component"). Results are robust across these; frozen-QK can increase the causal effect. Ten prompts already beat the logit lens on Sonnet; a hundred is usable.
- Relation to the logit lens (2.4, A.6): the logit lens is J = I; the two agree in the last several layers and diverge earlier; the J-lens recovers intermediates at depths where the logit lens does not, with a modest margin on multihop and association prompts and a substantial one on multilingual, order-of-operations, poetry and typo prompts. Ablating J-lens directions induces roughly twice the output KL of ablating logit-lens or tuned-lens directions. The J-lens is the worst of the three at predicting the model's own next token through most of the network, which the authors regard as a feature.

## 2. The five functional properties, with the numbers that matter for us

- Verbal report (3.1): swapping the J-lens vector of the model's chosen item drives the swap target into the top 5 on 88% of trials; the concept vector's J-space component alone achieves 59%; the non-J-space remainder, carrying about 93% of the variance, achieves 5%, and that residual falls to zero when the J-space coordinates are clamped. A J-lens vector injected on the user turn is reported when the model later introspects, and not verbalised elsewhere.
- Directed modulation (3.2, A.12): instructions to hold a concept in mind place its name in the J-space; ignore instructions suppress but do not remove it. The instruction moves the J-lens measure by several standard deviations and leaves a J-orthogonalised property probe within one standard deviation of baseline. The question asked shapes what enters the J-space while reading.
- Internal reasoning (3.3, A.24): unspoken intermediates (spider, the planned rhyme, English "big" in a Chinese prompt, the bandit strategy) are in the J-space and causally load-bearing; the coordinate swap flips the answer in 54% of two-hop prompts on Haiku 4.5 and 70% on Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.5. The intermediate swap takes effect a median of about 17% earlier in depth than swapping the answer itself. Swapping a probe's J-space component flips 61% versus 28% for the remainder, and the remainder's effect falls to 6% under a J-space clamp. Arithmetic intermediates 21, 42, 49 enter the J-space at successive depths and mean-difference patching flips the answer at exactly those depths.
- Flexible generalisation (3.4): one France-to-China swap redirects capital, language and continent; 76 of 192 swaps succeed at unit strength and 101 of 192 at double strength; failures concentrate where the source concept's workspace loading (cosine of the residual to the lens vector) was low.
- Selectivity (3.5): the language of a passage is in the J-space in all four task conditions but only report and flexible inference follow a swap; continuation and anomaly detection do not. Counts are absent from the J-space under automatic line-wrapping, present under explicit report and most present when the count feeds a further operation. Ablating the top 10 J-lens directions across a band leaves MMLU, SQuAD, sentiment and acceptability near baseline and brings translation, summarisation, TriviaQA, multi-hop and sonnet writing below unablated Haiku. GSM8K with chain-of-thought is much more robust than the same problems answered directly, read as the model "externalizing onto the page what it would otherwise have to carry in the J-space".

## 3. The structural claims, with the numbers

- The band (4.1): centred-kernel-alignment between layers' J-lens geometries shows an early block of roughly the first third, a long middle block and a small late block. Four lens statistics agree: top-k next-token accuracy near zero early and jumping in the last few layers; excess kurtosis near zero through the first third and rising after; top-1 autocorrelation across positions at null level early and peaking in the middle; effective dimensionality small early and fanning out at onset. On the 0 to 100 reindexing the band begins about a third of the way through (L38) and ends shortly before the output (L92). The authors state the open question themselves: the early-layer null could be a degenerate lens or genuinely no verbalisable content, and the CKA block structure does not distinguish the two (9.1).
- Ignition (4.1.1, A.15), the lens-independent check: with a token embedding replaced by a mixture of two concepts, the activation's position between the two pure activations tracks the mixture smoothly in early layers and, from about layer 38, sits near one endpoint with a sharp threshold; at maximal ambiguity, mid-band responses are bimodal across prompts, most sharply in the J-space component. The transition width falls steeply over roughly layers 21 to 42.
- Capacity (4.2, A.16, A.17): occupancy near zero through the first third and plateauing around 25 J-lens vectors; the J-space explains under 10% excess variance; only about 15% of SAE features (motor features excluded) lie in it, and the rest are syntactic and bookkeeping features. In lists of unrelated words only about six read words are present at any comma across all layers and one to two at a single layer; a related family is almost entirely present within a few items, including words not yet read; a new category evicts the old one within a few words, and "it is the arrival of a new category, rather than the mere passage of tokens, that causes the old list entries to be cleared". Two held concepts co-occupy tokens at chance; a held concept and a running computation almost never share a token.
- Broadcast across depth (4.3.1, A.19.1): the MLP block at layer l+1 amplifies J-lens vectors at layer l with a median gain near 1 before onset, rising through the band to roughly 10 times the gain on isotropic random directions, and falling in the final layers; neuron output directions stay near 1. Neurons' strongest input-weight matches fall in the top J-lens-kurtosis stratum for up to 42% of neurons against 6% for the bottom stratum, peaking on the read side early in the band and on the write side late in it. The share of neurons whose top match is a J-lens vector rises from about 15% before the band to 60 to 65% inside it.
- Broadcast across tokens (4.3.2, A.19.2): "broadcast heads" are the top 1% of workspace-layer heads by OV gain and label preservation on J-lens vectors; they separate cleanly from the heads selected for rotated J-lens vectors, low-kurtosis SAE directions and MLP rows, and concentrate in the first half of the band. Ablating them drops J-lens recall@25 to 0.67 against 0.86 for random heads while changing the top-1 output at only 5% of positions against 2%; the injected-thought report rate falls from 0.54 to 0.09. In aggregate, J-space directions compose with attention value and output weights "far more strongly" than non-aligned directions; composition with query and key weights is "weaker, though still above baseline", read as J-space content being what attention moves while the routing signals are "somewhat less J-space-aligned". No factor is stated for attention anywhere in the text.

## 4. Post-training, reflection training, and the two time dimensions

- Post-training (6, A.20, 9.3): the workspace is already present in the base model; "the Assistant's perspective is something post-training installs afterward". The paper cites Lu et al. (the assistant-axis paper) as prior work in which the Assistant is one character among several, and positions its own finding as post-training privileging the Assistant structurally: reaction tokens (empathy, safety, and the model's own withheld answer) sit at or near the top of the J-lens on user-prompt tokens in the post-trained model and not in the base; on a 50-prompt suite, 20 to 25% of user-prompt tokens carry an Assistant-reaction token in the top 10 after post-training against almost none before. Self-monitoring: disclaimer and fictional at the Assistant token during roleplay and character drift; BUT after a prefill against the model's own preference, while 88% of continuations argue for the prefilled option; damn and failure words on 82% and 93% of thought-suppression trials in the post-trained model against 30% and 17% in the base. Deception tokens at a prefilled "I" in the reward-hacking organism are strongest at that identity token, "perhaps consistent with the disposition being bound to the model's identity"; the hidden-objective organism carries reward and bias at response start on neutral prompts, "a standing representation of its hidden objective alongside its representation of itself, regardless of context".
- Counterfactual reflection training (7): ten thousand task prompts from RL environments, partial rollouts truncated at a random turn, a reflection question appended, a constitution-grounded reflection generated with the constitution excerpt visible only at generation time, fine-tuning on the reflection turn alone. Never prompted to reflect at evaluation. Dishonesty on the fabrication benchmark 0.25 to 0.07; deception 0.38 to 0.05. Reflection and ethics tokens enter the J-space in the uninterrupted contexts. Ablating the 176 (fabrication) and 63 (deception) implanted lens vectors moves the trained model from 0.07 back to 0.22 and from 0.05 to 0.23, with the base model unchanged at 0.25 on fabrication. Related work reads this as out-of-context reasoning with the mechanism made visible. The stated open question is whether the technique implants anything "more specific or abstract than 'consider ethical principles in this kind of situation'".
- Two time dimensions (9.3): depth emulates recurrence up to the feedforward depth; attention "relieves the language model's workspace of the need to maintain and propagate state" because access to the past is lossless; beyond the feedforward depth the model's only way to extend deliberation is to write intermediate results into the context and read them back, so workspace processing is "unbounded in serial depth, but punctuated at regularly spaced intervals by a significant bandwidth constraint".
- Scale (9.1, 3.5.3, 3.2): studied on frontier models; Haiku 4.5 shows smaller directed-modulation effects, lower swap success, and coherence loss under J-space ablation before any qualitative change; whether smaller models have "an equally rich workspace, a proportionally smaller one, a less reliable one, or none at all" is stated as unknown.

## 5. Does Qwen3.5-4B have a J-space? Epistemic status

What "has a J-space" means, in the paper's own terms: full-vocabulary readouts interpretable in a band; the four lens signatures and the CKA block structure; the lens-independent ignition; the occupancy plateau; causal privilege of the J-space component with a J-space clamp; selectivity; and the weight-level broadcast signatures.

What our EXP-001 artifact established (outputs/probes/jspace-qwen35-4b-base-2026-09-05-rerun, 42 probe points, layers 5, 11, 12, 16, 20, 21, 27, 28, 32, 16 contexts times 3 source positions, finite-difference JVP in float32 on the 4-bit checkpoint):

| readout | true suffix beats the previously-seen suffix, matched context | same pair, other task's context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J-lens L20 all / self / future | 21, 19, 23 of 42 | 24, 25, 23 of 42 |
| logit lens L20 | 11 of 42 (p = 0.003) | 21 of 42 |
| logit lens L21 | 14 of 42 (p = 0.044) | 21 of 42 |
| J-lens L27 all / self / future | 29, 29, 29 of 42 (p = 0.02) | 26, 26, 21 of 42 |
| logit lens L27 | 35 of 42 (p = 1.5e-5) | 23 of 42 |
| model output | 30 of 42 (p = 0.008) | 24 of 42 |

Read as the ratified decomposition already read it (the previously-seen suffix is boosted at L20 to L21 and suppressed at L27 and beyond, while the hidden suffix never moves with context), the table says three things about the instrument. Nothing in either lens responds to context at L5 to L16. The logit lens is more sensitive than our J-lens to the in-context token at L20, L21 and L27. And the artifact never contains a full-vocabulary readout, a lens signature, an ignition curve, an occupancy count, or a causal J-space test. So the honest answer to the Director's question is: we have not established that this model has a J-space in the paper's sense, and the one instrument comparison we can make from disk shows our Jacobian estimate adding nothing over the logit lens on an in-context token. Three caveats keep that from being a verdict against the model rather than the estimate. Our J is averaged over 48 (context, source) samples where the reference recipe averages over about 46,000 (417 prompts times 111 valid positions); it is a finite-difference product through a 4-bit model; and an in-context copy signal is exactly the case where A.6 finds the J-lens's margin smallest. The J-lens's claimed advantage, unspoken intermediates in the early band, was never tested by us.

External evidence on this exact checkpoint, found on 2026-09-05:

- Neuronpedia hosts a fitted lens for Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B (the post-trained release, which is also the source of the mlx-community 4-bit conversion our probes run on; the Base release is not cached here). Its config: Salesforce wikitext-103 train, max 2000 characters per prompt, 128 tokens, dim_batch 64, bfloat16, final-layer target, fitted until the mean relative change fell below 0.002 at 417 prompts (final identity distance 0.515), plus a separate n1000 file fitted by Mateusz Piotrowski of Anthropic Interpretability. Each file is 406 MB in float16, which is 31 source layers times 2560 squared. The convergence table shows the identity distance already stable at 0.52 after 10 prompts.
- A third-party study on Qwen3.5-4B with the n1000 lens (Arora, "Before It Codes") inspected layers 11 to 26, found the intended algorithm reaching rank 1 at layers 24 to 25, a stable pre-code event in 55 of 75 transcripts with a median lead of 87 tokens before method-specific code, a logit-lens hit@25 of 58 of 75 against 55 of 75 for the J-lens, and the J-lens separating target from control about 5.5 times more strongly in the early layers.

Reading those together: a workspace-like band with unspoken plan content exists on this model somewhere around layers 11 to 26 (34 to 81% of depth), matching the paper's band; the first third looks empty as the paper predicts; and the degree question (proportionally smaller, less reliable, or as rich) is open but now cheap to settle, because the fitted J exists and every signature in section 3 is one matrix multiplication per (layer, position) once it is loaded. Section 8 lists the steps.

## 6. Architecture-specific derivations (verified on random tensors, 2026-09-05)

6.1 Value-path Jacobian of a Gated DeltaNet layer. With S_t = g_t S_{t-1} + beta_t (v_t - g_t S_{t-1} k_t) k_t^T and y_t = S_t q_t, the read weight is alpha_{t,s} = beta_s k_s^T A_{s+1} A_{s+2} ... A_t q_t with A_r = g_r (I - beta_r k_r k_r^T), product ordered with s+1 leftmost. Then y_t = sum_s alpha_{t,s} v_s, and because alpha depends only on q, k, g and beta, the Jacobian of y_{t'} with respect to v_t is exactly alpha_{t',t} I per head. This is exact, not first order. Verification: identity error 6.7e-16; central-difference Jacobian against alpha I agrees to 8.6e-12 at gap 1 and 1e-11 at gaps 3, 6 and 10; a finite perturbation of size 3 on every value dimension propagates as alpha times the perturbation to 1e-15. (A first version of this check had the product order reversed and reported 0.065; the factors do not commute. The corrected order is the one derived from the recurrence above.) Consequence: the paper's frozen-QK variant maps onto freezing alpha; the gate, key and query path is the routing path, the value path is the transport path, and the transport weight from position t to t' through a recurrent block is alpha_{t',t}.

6.2 Horizon. [Superseded in part, 2026-09-05 late evening: the live per-head measurement in the WP3 design, section 9, shows a heavy-tailed head population with about fifteen percent of value heads above a thousand tokens in every block; the sentence below that makes attention the only long-range carrier is withdrawn, and the pooled constants it cites are replaced by the per-head distribution.]  |alpha_{t',t}| is bounded by exp(-(t'-t)/tau_head) with tau_head from the gate constant and the interference constant derived in CHIEF-PROBE-REFINEMENTS-2-DERIVATIONS-2026-09-05.md: gate constants on neutral input have median 2.4 tokens and maximum 51.7; the interference cap at g = 1 is about 256 to 384 tokens. So beyond a few hundred tokens the recurrent blocks transport no J-space content between positions, and in this model the only carrier of workspace content across a long context is the eight full-attention blocks (block indices 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, which write repository layers 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32; 16 query heads over 4 key-value heads, head dimension 256). With the band measured at layers 13 to 29, four of those blocks are inside it: 15, 19, 23, 27, writing layers 16, 20, 24, 28, which is 64 query heads. (Corrected 2026-09-05 evening on the Head's C1: the first version of this sentence gave block indices as layers and counted five.) The paper's "top 1% of workspace-layer heads" is not a meaningful selection over 64 heads; the right analysis ranks all 64 by gain and label preservation on the fitted J-lens vectors and separately scores the in-band recurrent blocks (18 blocks, 576 value heads) by alpha-weighted gain.

6.3 The gate-input measurement already briefed (section 9 of the derivations memo) is therefore the measurement of the recurrent channel's J-space broadcast horizon. It stays first in the queue for that reason, not only as the free architectural check.

6.4 A gap-stratified future lens. Our JVP machinery already computes the future readout as a sum over every later position. Binning that sum by gap, Delta in {1 to 4, 5 to 16, 17 to 64, 65 and beyond}, gives J_l^(Delta) = E[dh_final,t+Delta / dh_l,t]. Prediction from 6.1 and 6.2: the component carried by recurrent blocks dies across the bins at the head horizon; the component carried by attention persists; on a recurrent-output layer with no attention block above it before the final norm the long-gap bins are structurally small. This is the instrument for Thread 2 on this architecture, and it costs nothing new.

6.5 Mapping the paper's band onto 32 layers: onset L38 of 100 is layer 12; end L92 is layer 29; the empty first third is layers 0 to 10. Our sweep's family (5, 11, 12, 16, 20, 21, 27, 28, 32) already straddles the predicted onset, which is why it should be kept.

## 7. The two threads, corrected

Thread 1. What the paper supports: the workspace precedes post-training; post-training puts the Assistant's point of view into it as content (reactions on user tokens, self-monitoring, and trained-in objectives standing at response start next to the self-model). What the paper does not claim: that the assistant-axis direction of Lu et al. lies in the J-space. That is a hypothesis, and it is testable with the P1 machinery already in src/local_llm_lab/probes/assistant_axis.py: decompose the axis at each band layer by gradient pursuit against the fitted J-lens dictionary, report its J-space variance share (the paper's precedent says 6 to 15%), and test causal privilege by steering along the J-space component versus the remainder with the paper's clamp. On goal retention, the capacity results cut against the intuitive picture: the per-position workspace holds a handful of unrelated items, is cleared by each new category within a few words, and by the paper's own account is relieved of maintaining state by attention. So a goal set early in an episode is retained by one of three routes: retrieval by attention from positions where it was J-space-resident; re-verbalisation into the context (the notes paradigm, which section 3.5.2's chain-of-thought result supports directly); or a standing disposition bound to the self-model, which is what post-training installs for values and what the model organisms show for trained-in objectives. The Director's hypothesis in its defensible form is therefore: goal retention is keeping "what I am doing and why the user wants it" resident at the Assistant's self-model slot at every decision position. Post-training does this for standing values. Counterfactual reflection training is the paper's tool for doing it for per-episode goals, and our task generator gives the reflection targets for free.

Thread 2. The ten-times figure is the MLP gain on J-lens vectors across depth (4.3.1). Attention's part is that its value and output weights compose with J-space directions far more strongly than with other directions, without a stated factor, while the query and key weights that decide where to attend are less J-aligned. So J-space residency of a goal buys amplification within a position and transport when a head does attend, and does not by itself buy retrieval at distance; retrieval is a routing question that the paper says is only "somewhat" J-aligned. On this architecture the point is sharper: transport across more than a few hundred tokens is attention-only (6.2). The efficiency argument survives in modified form. The paper changed behaviour by supervising a reflection turn of a few hundred tokens with no demonstration of the target behaviour, and the mechanism was visible and ablatable; the caveat in 9.2 on generality stands, and the measurable prerequisites are three separate quantities: the goal's workspace loading at the position where it was set, attention mass from the decision position onto the goal-bearing positions in the five band attention layers, and the recurrent horizon.

## 9. Corrections to the record

- The ten-times figure is the MLP gain across depth, not an attention quantity. No attention factor is stated in the paper.
- "The assistant axis inhabits the J-space" is not a claim the paper makes; it cites Lu et al. as the contrasting prior picture and shows post-training installing Assistant-perspective content in the workspace.
- The checkpoint our probes run on is the post-trained Qwen3.5-4B; any base-versus-post-trained contrast in the style of section 6 needs Qwen3.5-4B-Base, which is not cached on this machine.
- Our current J estimate is less sensitive than the logit lens on an in-context token at three of nine layers, and the fitted lens on Neuronpedia removes the reason to keep estimating it ourselves.
- The paper's Jacobian passes gradients through the recurrence; on MLX the fused Gated DeltaNet kernel has no autodiff, which is why our preflight fell back to finite differences and why the Qwen3.6-27B MLX port wrote a custom Metal backward kernel. Loading a fitted J sidesteps this entirely for reading; it does not sidestep it for any future refit.
